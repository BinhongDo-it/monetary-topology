# B28 results: the posted number is defended, and which margin gives way depends on the tier

Criteria in [`b28_prereg.md`](b28_prereg.md). **B28-1 is read below. B28-2
through B28-6 are not yet run.**

---

## B28-1. The discount is a posted rule, and the rule is exact

Seven items carrying both a refurbished price and an original price on the
maker's own listing, spanning two product categories and a `3.42` factor in list
price.

| item | refurbished | original | `P4/P1` | `0.85 x P1` | rounded down to a nine-ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| phone, 512GB | 1,019 | 1,199 | 0.84987 | 1,019.15 | **1,019** |
| phone, 1TB | 1,269 | 1,499 | 0.84656 | 1,274.15 | **1,269** |
| desktop, base | 1,269 | 1,499 | 0.84656 | 1,274.15 | **1,269** |
| desktop, upper | 1,439 | 1,699 | 0.84697 | 1,444.15 | **1,439** |
| laptop 14 | 1,699 | 1,999 | 0.84992 | 1,699.15 | **1,699** |
| laptop 16, mid | 2,549 | 2,999 | 0.84995 | 2,549.15 | **2,549** |
| laptop 16, top | 3,479 | 4,099 | 0.84874 | 3,484.15 | **3,479** |

**The rule is: take fifteen per cent off the list price, then round down to the
nearest price ending in nine. Seven of seven fit it exactly.**

`P4/P1` spans `0.84656` to `0.84995`, a range of `0.40` per cent, log sd
`0.00177`. **Across a phone and a workstation, and across a factor of `3.42` in
price.**

**B28-1 returns its first state without ambiguity.** The discount clusters on one
value across models of different age, specification and category, so it is a
posted convention rather than anything about the object.

## B28-1, corrected on a larger sample: the discount is stratified by line, not flat

**The seven-item reading above is not general and this is stated rather than left
to be discovered.** Twenty-three items from a third product family, all carrying a
refurbished price and a stated saving, give `P4/P1` from `0.8189` to `0.8874`,
**log sd `0.0203`, eleven times the seven-item figure**, and the fifteen-per-cent
rounding rule fits only three of the twenty-three.

**The dispersion is not noise. It sorts by product line.**

| line | n | mean `P4/P1` | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| entry | 2 | 0.8448 | 0.8398 – 0.8497 |
| small | 6 | 0.8551 | 0.8497 – 0.8587 |
| mid | 5 | 0.8544 | 0.8189 – 0.8856 |
| **professional** | **10** | **0.8769** | 0.8666 – 0.8874 |

**The professional line is discounted least, and every one of its ten items sits
above the entry line's whole range.** Within that line the ratio holds a narrow
band across a near doubling in price, from the smallest configuration to the
largest.

**So B28-1's first state stands and its content changes.** The discount is still
a posted convention carrying no information about the item: within a line it is
nearly constant across configurations and sizes. **It is not one convention for
the firm. It is one convention per line.**

**The seven-item result is now readable as one line that happens to run on an
exact rule**, rather than as a firm-wide rule, and the corrected claim is the
weaker and better supported one.

---

## Why the professional line would be discounted least

**A deep discount teaches buyers that the full price is not real.** What a
refurbished price risks is not margin on the unit but the standing of the number
it is derived from. **A line whose price premium rests on the label rather than on
capability has the most to lose from that**, so it can afford the least discount.

**That predicts an inverse relation and it is testable**: discount depth should
fall as the share of a line's premium carried by the label rather than by
capability rises.

**The professional tablet line is the case where that share is largest**, because
the software layer caps what the hardware may do, so successive silicon
generations converge in what the device can actually be used for. **That
capability assessment is a judgment and is recorded as one**; the software cap
itself is documented, the parity across generations is not measured here, and
B28-6 is the arm that would read the price side of it.

**Note the direction, because it is the opposite of the intuitive one.** The line
whose object least supports its price is the line whose price is protected
hardest.

### Scope of the reading

**It applies where there is margin to give up.** A shallow discount on a
thin-margin good is the absence of room, not the protection of an anchor, and the
two are distinct causes of the same observation. **The scope is stated here so
the reading is not carried outside it.**

**The carrier must therefore be one whose margin is separately known to be
large**, which the present one is. **On a thin-margin seller the reading is
unavailable.**

**The two accounts are separable, and by the sign.** Anchor protection predicts
shallow discounts where the premium over cost is **largest**. Absence of room
predicts shallow discounts where the margin is **smallest**. **They disagree on
the sign of the relation between gross margin and discount depth**, so a panel
spanning sellers of different margin decides between them, and neither is assumed
here.

**Until that panel exists, this section is an interpretation of a within-seller
pattern and is labelled as one.** What is measured is the line stratification.
Why the lines differ is not.

---

## What the reading is, beyond the number

**Two conventions are stacked and neither carries information about the item.**
One is the per-line discount, exactly fifteen per cent on the line read first. The other is the rounding to a nine-ending price
point, which is the price-point lattice documented in the rigidity literature
cited in [`b26_results.md`](b26_results.md). **The residual variation in `P4/P1`
is entirely the second convention acting on the first.**

**The seller certifies the object and computes the price.** The same listing
advertises "a one-year warranty, full functional testing, and savings up to 15%".
**The warranty half asserts the object is equivalent to new. The fifteen per cent
half is now shown to be arithmetic on the list price.** So the price gap is not a
measurement of the object; it is a function of the name's list price.

**Consequence, and it is what B28-5 will test.** If `P4` is a fixed function of
`P1`, the maker cannot let `P4` follow an ageing model's market value without
breaking a posted rule. **The remaining margin is whether the model is listed at
all**, and the catalogue read on the same date carried only the two most recent
phone models and no earlier generation. **That is consistent with existence being
the adjustment margin and is not a test of it**; B28-5 states the test.

**This is the third carrier in the project showing the same shape.** A posted
number is rigid and the adjustment happens somewhere behind it: coverage and
eligibility in the prepared beverage carrier, storage content in the price band
carrier, and here, on present evidence, the existence of the listing.

---

## The mechanism has a second side, and until now every instance was on one of them

**Every posted-price instance in this project so far is the same mismatch.** A
posted number sits above what the market will pay, and the seller adjusts
something other than the number: eligibility and frequency in the prepared
beverage carrier, storage content in the price band carrier, and the existence of
the listing here. **All three are the too-high corner.**

**A mechanism observed only on one side of a mismatch invites the reading that it
is selective observation.** The other corner is therefore worth naming, and one
category supplies it.

**Top-tier handbags trade on the secondary market above their retail price.** Two
independent resale platforms' annual reports agree on the direction: one puts a
leading maker's brand-level retention at `138` per cent and one model at `282`,
the other reports the flagship model's resale value rising year on year.
**Direction is all that is used here and the reason is given below.**

**The posted retail price is therefore below the clearing price, and the maker
does not raise it to clear.** It rations the quantity instead. **Raising the
posted price is available and is not taken**, exactly as repricing an ageing
listing is available and is not taken.

**So the shape is two-sided.** The posted number is rigid against a mismatch in
either direction, and what differs is which margin absorbs it: **existence and
coverage when the number is too high, quantity when it is too low.**

**Evidence status, and this is why only the direction is used.** The handbag
figures come from resale platforms' own annual reports, which are marketing
documents from interested parties and disclose neither sample size nor
geographic coverage. **A retention-level comparison against a category that
retains almost nothing is not made**, and the reason is structural rather than a
matter of effort.

### The category that retains nothing has no anchor to lose, and that is a scope
### condition rather than a data gap

**The mechanism needs a name whose posted price persists across units.** An anchor
is inherited by later units of the same name, which is what makes it an anchor
rather than a transaction.

**High-end tailoring is not priced that way.** Garments are priced individually,
one tag per piece, and there is no posted figure attached to a cut or a model that
successive pieces inherit. **So there is no name-level number for a secondary
market to reference.**

**That converts the missing data into a prediction rather than a hole.** Where no
name-level price is posted, the mechanism has nothing to attach to, the secondary
market has nothing to reference, and prices should fall back onto the object. **For
a fitted garment the object's transferable value is close to nothing, which is
what is observed.**

### Information is a fourth margin, and the discriminating case is in this station

**Publishing a retention figure would tell buyers what the posted price is worth,
which is the same damage a deep discount does.** So where a party controls such a
figure and its anchor would suffer from it, the figure is not published. **That
is the same class of act as removing an edge or choosing a factor, applied to
information rather than to price or quantity.**

**This account is not free, because one seller in this station does publish a
number of exactly that kind.** The maker posts trade-in values per model, and a
trade-in value is a statement about what a used unit fetches.

**The resolution is in the currency, and it is checkable.** The trade-in value is
paid in purchase credit or a gift card and **cannot be taken as cash**. It is
therefore not a price the used unit commands in money; it is a discount on a new
purchase, denominated in a unit that only spends in one place. **A number that
looks like a valuation and is denominated in a currency that does not cash out is
not a valuation.**

**So the fourth margin is available and constrained.** Publishing a figure is
avoided where it would price the anchor in money, and permitted where the figure
is denominated so that it cannot. **The falsifiable form: where a maker publishes
its own buy-back number, that number should be payable in a restricted currency
far more often than in cash.**

**Stated as scope: this station reads categories in which a name carries a posted
price.** Per-item pricing is outside it, and being outside it is the reason that
category behaves the way it does. **The falsifiable form is that resale dispersion
relative to any notional model should be far wider in per-item-priced categories
than in name-priced ones**, which is measurable without needing a retention level
from either side.

**Two object-level confounds are named rather than argued away.** A fitted
garment loses utility on transfer in a way a handbag does not, which is a real
property of the object and not of any anchor. And rationing has its own
explanations that owe nothing to this account. **What the corner supplies is that
the number does not move, not why the quantity is capped.**

---

## What this does not establish

**Does not establish** anything about the market price. `P2` has not been
collected and no comparison to it is made here.

**Does not establish** that existence is the adjustment margin. The catalogue
observation is one date and one product line, and B28-5 is the arm that would test
it.

**Does not establish** that the fifteen per cent is unfair or mispriced. The
finding is that it is computed rather than assessed.

**Does not establish** that the rule holds for other makers or other categories.
Seven items from one maker, two categories, one date.

# B28-5a results: the tier whose posted price never moves is the tier the maker removes

**Read 2026-08-30, under the instrument correction recorded in the prereg the same
day.** Catalogue presence was the wrong quantity. The maker does not control
whether an object goes on trading, and a shop window emptying says nothing about
whether the model still exists to be priced. **Stop of sale is controlled by the
maker and is announced**, and it is what this arm now reads.

## The instrument, and how much slack it has

The maker publishes the rule: vintage is *"stopped distributing them for sale more
than 5 and less than 7 years ago"*, obsolete is *"more than 7 years ago"*. So the
vintage list is a deterministic relabelling of the stop-of-sale date and carries
no timing information of its own. **It is used here only to check the compiled
dates, never as an independent observation.**

Four rows check out exactly. iPad Pro 11-inch first generation and iPad Pro
12.9-inch third generation both stop on 2020-03-18 in the compilation, which puts
them vintage from 2025-03 and obsolete from 2027-03; both sit on the vintage list
on 2026-08-30. iPad Air third generation (2020-09-15) and iPad sixth generation
(2019-09-10) likewise.

**Three rows do not, and the failure is in the list.** iPad Pro 10.5-inch, iPad
Air first generation Cellular and the 2012 cellular iPad all stopped selling more
than seven years ago and are still carried as vintage rather than obsolete. The
maker's page states region-dependent handling elsewhere on the same page, and one
Beats block is explicitly labelled vintage in one country and obsolete in others.
**So the list is a lower bound on age, not a clock**, and the compiled dates are
reported at their own tier rather than upgraded by this check.

## Panel one, tablets: dispersion by line

Sale life is stop-of-sale minus release. Overlap is stop-of-sale minus the
successor's release inside the same line: positive means the maker kept the older
model on sale after its replacement existed.

| line | n | sale life min | max | max/min | sd(log) | models kept past successor | longest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPad | 10 | 7.3 | 36.2 | 4.99 | 0.5388 | 2 | 24.0 |
| iPad mini | 6 | 10.6 | 42.3 | 3.99 | 0.5155 | 2 | 28.9 |
| iPad Air | 7 | 9.6 | 28.9 | 3.01 | 0.4381 | 1 | 17.0 |
| **iPad Pro** | **7** | **12.8** | **18.8** | **1.46** | **0.1242** | **0** | **-0.2** |

Months throughout. The professional line is three and a half to four times tighter
in log dispersion than any other line in the same family, and **its overlap is
never positive: seven transitions, every one of them a withdrawal within about a
week before the successor shipped.**

**The five models kept past their own successor are all outside the professional
line**, and each was kept at a reduced number:

| model | months kept past successor | what happened to its price |
|---|---|---|
| iPad 2 | 24.0 | cut from $499 to $399 at the successor's launch |
| iPad mini 2 | 28.9 | *"offered at now more affordable prices"*, $299 |
| iPad mini (1st) | 19.2 | same sentence, $249 |
| iPad Air (1st) | 17.0 | same sentence, $399 |
| iPad (9th) | 18.4 | held at $329 while the successor launched at $449 |

**Three of the five are named in one sentence of the maker's own press release**,
2014-10-16: *"iPad Air, iPad mini 2 and iPad mini, available in silver or space
gray, are offered at now more affordable prices."* That is an official statement
that the maker's response to a successor, on the non-professional lines, is to
keep the object and lower the number.

**The fifth case is the interesting one and it runs the other way.** The ninth
generation was not cut; it was held at $329 while the tenth launched at $449.
Nineteen months later, on 2024-05-07, the maker discontinued the ninth and cut the
tenth to $349. **The excursion to $449 was undone and the surviving number landed
within $20 of the old anchor**, which is the anchor argument running in one line
of a product catalogue.

goes.**

## The supply story does not survive the within-generation cut

The obvious competing reading is that the professional model leaves because its
components are retired or its demand collapses, and pricing has nothing to do with
it. **The 2022 event settles this.** The 13 Pro was withdrawn at 11.4 months while
the iPhone 13, launched the same day, from the same generation, on the same supply
chain, stayed on sale for 35.5 months, cut to $699 in 2022 and to $599 in 2023. A supply or demand story
predicts the same treatment for both. It does not predict a split by tier.

The dispersion figures say the same thing more weakly and over more cases: a
supply story would put the professional line's sale life at least as dispersed as
the others, since components are the shared input. It is four times tighter.

## What this joins

**B28-1 measured the other side of the same decision.** The refurbished discount is
stratified by line, and the professional line carries the *smallest* discount,
0.8769 against 0.8448 at the entry line. So the one channel in which the maker
could mark a professional unit down is the channel in which it marks it down
least. Here the maker declines to mark it down at all and removes it. **Two arms,
two instruments, same ordering: the higher the tier, the harder the posted number
is defended.**

**B26 measured rigidity at a focal number while the cost moved.** This is rigidity
at a posted number while the object ages, and it uses the fourth margin that
station did not have: existence. The list from B26 was coverage, content and
information. **Existence completes it, and it is the margin the maker reaches for
exactly where the other three are unavailable.**

## The one time the professional number moved

The maker's professional starting price was $999 from 2017 to 2024, eight
generations. It moved once, on 2025-09-09, to $1,099, and the maker's own release
records what moved with it: *"iPhone 17 Pro will be available in double the entry
storage, 256GB"*. **The step was again $100, and it came with the content margin
moving in the same act**, which is the B25 pattern read forward: the number and
what stands behind it are adjusted together rather than the number alone.

## Scope

The claim is about **the maker's own posted price in one market**. Carrier and
reseller discounts on withdrawn professional models exist and are outside it, and
a trade report on the 2024 withdrawal says exactly that such units remained buyable
elsewhere. That is a fact about other sellers' prices, which is the object of a
different station.

Tablet dates are a third-party compilation, checked against the maker's own list
on four rows with three known failures in the list, all recorded above. Phone dates
are trade press on the day of the event for 2024 and 2025 and third-party
infoboxes for the four earlier generations. **The pricing statements for the
tablets are official.**

**What would break the reading.** A professional model kept on sale by the maker
after its successor launched, at a reduced posted price. None occurs in thirteen
professional transitions across two product families. One such case would not be
fatal, but a rate approaching the non-professional lines' would be.

# B28-5b results: the maker runs two clocks, and only one of them is set by tier

**The prereg named three published instruments and warned that two of them are the
same observation.** Hardware service withdrawal is, by the maker's own stated
rule, a fixed function of the stop of sale, so it inherits whatever the stop of
sale does and cannot check it. **Software support withdrawal is the one that can
disagree**, because it is set at a silicon boundary rather than at a launch event.
This arm is the disagreement test, and it is the arm that could have destroyed
B28-5a.

**The test is only available where support has already ended.** Every model from
2019 onward is still receiving the current release, so its support life is
censored. That leaves the two cohorts whose boundary has landed, and both of them
contain a matched pair: same year, same maker, same family, same chip generation,
different tier.

| cohort | model | tier | sale life (mo) | last major OS | OS generations received | support life (mo) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | iPhone X | professional | 10.28 | iOS 16 | 6 | 58.28 |
| 2017 | iPhone 8 | standard | 30.75 | iOS 16 | 6 | 59.66 |
| 2018 | iPhone XS | professional | 11.63 | iOS 18 | 7 | 71.85 |
| 2018 | iPhone XR | standard | 34.63 | iOS 18 | 7 | 70.70 |

**Within each cohort the sale life differs by a factor of almost exactly three
(2.99 and 2.98) and the support life differs by about one month (ratios 1.024 and
0.984).** That one month is the gap between the two models' own launch dates, so
on the quantity that matters, the number of major releases each device receives,
the pair is identical: six and six, seven and seven.

**This is the reading.** The maker operates two withdrawal clocks over the same
object. The engineering clock is set by the chip, crosses the tier boundary, and
treats a $999 model and a $699 model of the same year as one thing. The commercial
clock is set by tier, and separates them threefold. **The professional model is not
withdrawn because it has run out of technical life. It is withdrawn while it still
has four to six more years of support ahead of it**, in the case of the iPhone X,
withdrawn 48 months before its last operating system shipped.

**It also kills the strongest remaining competing reading of B28-5a.** A referee
can say that the professional model is the technically leading product and is
therefore superseded fastest. That story predicts a shorter technical life for the
professional model. Its technical life is the same, to within the launch-date
offset, in both cohorts where the comparison is available.

**And it settles which of the two announcements is informative.** Of the two
withdrawal announcements, the hardware one is downstream of the pricing decision by
construction, and the software one is independent of it. They behave exactly as
that split predicts, which is a consistency check on the instrument set rather
than a second finding.

**Limits.** Two matched pairs, because two is how many exist: no earlier cohort
has a professional tier and no later cohort has an ended support boundary. The
comparison is matched on the variables that a supersession story would use, and
the direction was written down before the dates were collected. **What would break
it:** a cohort in which the professional model's support ends materially earlier
than its standard sibling's. The 2019 and 2020 cohorts will test this when their
boundaries land, and they are recorded here as pending rather than as support.

- iOS 26 compatibility reporting, for the iPhone XR and XS support boundary
- iOS 16 released 2022-09-12 and iOS 18 released 2024-09-16 are used as the support-life endpoints

# B28-6: the new name does not reset the anchor, and the arm returns its second branch

**Read 2026-08-30**, against the criterion fixed in
[`b28_prereg.md`](b28_prereg.md) §3 and not edited after. **An earlier computation
of this arm was performed and never written to a file; the series has been
recollected from sources rather than reconstructed, and it reproduces the earlier
figures exactly**, which is the only reason those figures are mentioned at all.

## The criterion returns its second branch

**Registered:** wide dispersion means each name carries an independently set
anchor; clustering near one means the new name inherits the old anchor and the
independence reading fails.

**Median exactly 1.0000, six of fourteen transitions exactly equal, log sd 0.0825.
That is clustering.**

**So a new name does not reset the anchor on this line, and the independence
reading fails.** This is a negative result on a hypothesis this project would have
liked, and it is the same direction as the ladder finding in
[`b25`](b25_price_band_availability.md): the rungs and the spacing persist across
fourteen years and a full turnover of product generations, while the names change
every year. **The name is renewed annually and the number is not.**

## The one stretch where the price moved, and what it did

Four consecutive non-zero moves, 2018 through 2021:

```
+20.62%   -15.39%   +14.55%   -4.76%
```

**Signs strictly alternating. Amplitude strictly decreasing.** And what follows is
**three consecutive exact zeros**.

**That is what a system settling onto a fixed point looks like**, and it is the
only stretch of four consecutive non-zero moves in the series.

**Two cautions, stated because this pattern is more attractive than its evidence.**
With four terms, strict alternation and strict monotone decay would arise together
by chance in roughly half a per cent of random orderings, which sounds strong until
one notices that **the window was chosen as the stretch where the price moved**,
and a stretch selected for having moves is not a random draw. **Nothing here is
tested.** The pattern is recorded as an observation and the arm does not rest on
it.

## The two readings of this line are not two pieces of evidence

**The prereg requires this reading and B25's to be reported together, and the
reason is now visible.** B25 reads this line by year and gets a log sd of 0.0805.
This arm reads it by generation and gets 0.0825. **These are two cuts of one
series, not two independent confirmations**, and the near-equality of the two
figures is arithmetic rather than corroboration. **Any argument that leans on both
is double counting.**

## Limits

1. **One line, one country, one maker.** The prereg asks for this quantity on every
   line the maker publishes; only this one has been read.
2. **The middle of the series rests on a single compilation** dated 2022-10-16.
   The two endpoints are independently sourced and the 2023 entry is separately
   reported; **the ten values in between are one source deep.**
3. **The line definition excludes the premium model**, which makes the 2017 to 2018
   step consecutive. A different line definition would change that step and with it
   the largest positive move in the table. **The definition is stated rather than
   defended**, and a reader who prefers another one should expect a different
   number there.

### The seller's own claim, located, and it does not bound the measured rule

**Source: apple.com/shop/refurbished, read 2026-08-30. Official.** Verbatim:

> *"Apple Certified Refurbished products include a one-year warranty, full
> functional testing, and savings up to 15%"*

with *"Free delivery and returns"* alongside. **That sentence is the whole basis on
which [`b28_prereg.md`](b28_prereg.md) §1 treats the refurbished unit and the new
unit as the same object**, so it is the file's most load-bearing quotation and it
is now sourced.

**The discrepancy.** *"Up to 15%"* reads as a ceiling. The measured panel does not
respect it as one.

| quantity | ratio `P4/P1` | implied saving |
|---|---|---|
| advertised ceiling | 0.8500 | **15.00%** |
| measured maximum saving | 0.8189 | **18.11%** |
| measured minimum saving | 0.8874 | 11.26% |
| entry line mean | 0.8448 | **15.52%** |
| professional line mean | 0.8769 | 12.31% |

**The advertised 15% sits inside the measured range rather than above it**, and the
entry line's mean saving already exceeds it. **So the headline number is not the
rule.** It is a round figure in the shop window, and the rule underneath is
stratified by line and crosses it in both directions.

**This strengthens rather than weakens the arm's reading.** The first pass on seven
items found them fitting `floor(0.85 x P1)` rounded to a nine-ending exactly, which
is the advertised rate. **The larger panel broke that**, and now the advertised
rate is identified as what it is: a focal number that some items land on and the
line-level rule straddles. **The seller posts one number and prices by another**,
which is the same shape as every other finding in this file.

**No complaint is implied.** A saving larger than advertised harms nobody and
"up to" is not a promise of a maximum in ordinary usage. **The point is only that
the posted figure cannot be used as the rule**, and a reading that took 15% as the
rule would have been wrong on twenty of twenty-three items.
