# B150: Singapore's foreign worker levy, one job priced by worker class and by the employer's tier

Script: `experiments/b150_levy_table.py` (with `--selftest`). Record: `results/b150_levy_table.json`.
Criteria are written in the script, the record carries their text, and every quantity the run produces is in the record.

## 1. Object

Singapore's Ministry of Manpower charges employers a monthly levy for each Work Permit and S Pass holder.
The levy is written on two indices at once.

- **Class**: higher-skilled Work Permit holder (HS), basic-skilled Work Permit holder (BS), construction's unskilled class (U), S Pass holder (SP).
  Moving between HS and BS takes a skills certificate, or a salary and years-of-work threshold that each sector page sets for itself; only the worker and the employer can make that move.
- **Position**: in services and manufacturing, the levy tier a worker falls into, set by the employer's own share of migrant workers in its total workforce
  (services: Tier 1 up to 10%, Tier 2 above 10% to 25%, Tier 3 above 25% to 35%; manufacturing: up to 25%, above 25% to 50%, above 50% to 60%).
  In construction, the source group (Malaysia, NAS and PRC; non-traditional sources, NTS) and the off-site construction row. In process, the source group. Marine shipyard has one cell.

Price: the monthly levy per worker, SGD. Every number comes from the five sector pages, the S Pass levy and quota page,
the S Pass changes page (the rates before 1 September 2025), and the Ministry's guide "How to calculate your quota and levy bill" (updated 1 July 2026).
The pages carry no date in their address and are edited in place, so the run reads text snapshots of the seven pages taken on 14 October 2026,
each listed with its address, the page's own "Last updated" date, a SHA-256 of the stored text, and whether the stored text
transcribes the page in full (five pages), in part (manufacturing: table headers paraphrased, figures and quoted sentences as printed)
or as a summary (the S Pass changes page: the quoted sentence as printed, the tables summarised).

## 3. Squares

For two positions a and b, the square is `(BS − HS)@a − (BS − HS)@b`, read in SGD (floor $1) and in logs (`log(BS/HS)` differences, floor `log((x+1)/x)` with `x` the smallest of the four numbers).
A sign is read only where both compositions are nonzero and agree.

| Square | SGD | log (multiple of floor) | Sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Services Tier 1 vs Tier 2 | −50 | 0 | not read (one composition zero) |
| Services Tier 1 vs Tier 3 | −50 | +0.1178 (35.4) | not read (opposite signs) |
| Services Tier 2 vs Tier 3 | 0 | +0.1178 (47.2) | not read (one composition zero) |
| Manufacturing Tier 1 vs Tier 2 | 0 | +0.0972 (24.4) | not read (one composition zero) |
| Manufacturing Tier 1 vs Tier 3 | +20 | +0.2250 (56.4) | + |
| Manufacturing Tier 2 vs Tier 3 | +20 | +0.1277 (44.8) | + |
| Construction Malaysia, NAS, PRC vs off-site | +280 | +0.4553 (114.0) | + |
| Construction NTS vs off-site | +280 | +0.1957 (49.0) | + |
| Construction Malaysia, NAS, PRC vs NTS | 0 | +0.2595 (78.0) | not read (one composition zero) |
| Process Malaysia, NAS, PRC vs NTS | −100 | +0.0377 (7.6) | not read (opposite signs) |

These are all ten squares between two positions of the same table. Each table's squares are generated by `b1 = E − V + 1` independent ones: two in services, manufacturing and construction, one in process, seven in all; in each three-position table one square is the difference of the other two.
Every nonzero square stands 20 to 280 floors from zero in SGD and 7.6 to 114 floors in logs; the zeros are differences of integers and exactly zero.
The two unskilled squares between the on-site source groups are one object: the unskilled rate is 900 in both groups, so each SGD square
equals the change of the other class's rate between the groups, 200 for both, and their difference is the zero square above.
Where a sign is read, table by table: the basic-skilled premium in manufacturing narrows in Tier 3 (SGD 120, 120, 100; ratio 1.480, 1.343, 1.182), and the premium on construction sites is wider than off site.

Three standard shapes for a levy each predict something about these tables, read table by table:

| Shape | Prediction | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| One value per position | one number per sector or tier | two values at every one of the 12 positions |
| Class term plus position term | every SGD square of a table zero | holds in none of the four tables |
| Class term times position term | every log square of a table zero | holds in none of the four tables |

Single squares where a shape's zero holds: SGD zero on services Tier 2 vs Tier 3, manufacturing Tier 1 vs Tier 2 and construction Malaysia, NAS, PRC vs NTS; log zero on services Tier 1 vs Tier 2. How many single squares are zero depends on which squares are taken as a basis, so the count is read on whole tables.

## 4. The bill for one more worker

The guide's steps fix the bill: the maximum number of migrant workers is `floor(LQS count × 0.538462)` in services and `× 1.5` in manufacturing;
S Pass holders may be at most 10% (services) or 15% (manufacturing) of the total workforce `W`;
`Tier 1 = floor(a·W)`, `Tier 2 = floor(b·W) − Tier 1`, the rest in Tier 3, with `(a, b) = (10%, 25%)` in services and `(25%, 50%)` in manufacturing;
workers fill the tiers in the order S Pass holders, then higher-skilled, then basic-skilled.

Over every mix with LQS count 1 to 80, S Pass holders 0 to 7, and HS and BS 0 to 24 each, keeping only mixes within quota before and after the hire,
the increase in the monthly bill from one more worker, computed from these steps, takes these values:

| Sector | One more S Pass | One more HS | One more BS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Services | 650, 750, 800, 850, 950, 1,000 | 300, 400, 450, 500, 600, 650 | 450, 500, 600, 650, 700, 800 |
| Manufacturing | 650, 750, 930, 950 | 250, 350, 530, 550 | 350, 370, 450, 470, 650 |

The sets are the same with LQS count 1 to 40, with LQS count 1 to 80 in half steps (a local employee earning $900 to $1,800 counts as half),
and on a grid wide enough to hold every mix the quota allows (services: LQS count 1 to 80, S Pass 0 to 14, HS and BS 0 to 45;
manufacturing: LQS count 1 to 40, S Pass 0 to 16, HS and BS 0 to 61). The values are the combinations of the two tier boundaries one
more S Pass holder can move: $650 plus 0, 100, 150, 200, 300 or 350 in services, and plus 0, 100, 280 or 300 in manufacturing.
The S Pass page's sentence "harmonised to $650 across all sectors and levy tiers" is true of the printed rate.
On the bill, one more S Pass holder costs $650 in some mixes and up to $1,000 in others, because S Pass holders take the tier slots first and push a work permit holder already on the payroll into a higher tier.
The record lists the number of grid cells behind every value; those are cells of the grid, not shares of employers.

## 5. The guide's worked examples

The guide's three worked bills, recomputed from its own steps and its own rate table:

| Example | Printed | Recomputed |
|---|---|---|
| Factory, page 12 | 14,430 | 14,430 |
| Café, page 14 | 1,300 | 1,300 |
| Factory, page 15 | 13,280 | 13,760 |

On page 15 the Tier 2 line charges four Malaysian basic-skilled workers $350 each; the guide's own list of that workforce makes them basic-skilled, and $350 is the Tier 2 higher-skilled rate. The difference is $480.
The guide's rate table also prints $300 for a marine shipyard higher-skilled worker, where the marine page prints $350 (with a daily rate of $11.51, which matches $350).
The other 25 rate cells of the guide agree with the pages.
These four rows are errors in the Ministry's guide itself, the source document: on page 15 the guide contradicts its own steps, and in its rate table it contradicts the marine page. They are not errors in this reading.

## 6. Announced for 2028

Services and manufacturing will merge Tier 1 and Tier 2 (merged tier: services 400 / 600, manufacturing 300 / 470); process raises the basic-skilled rates to 600 and 800; marine raises basic-skilled to 600; the construction page announces nothing.
The pages say the implementation timeline will be released in due course. The announced tables are in the record, marked as announced.
With Tier 3 at its current rates, which the pages do not restate for 2028, the announced services table has every SGD square zero, and manufacturing keeps a +70 square between the merged tier and Tier 3.
The criterion that compares the pages with the announcement (L3-6) is read in the month the change takes effect; until then it carries no verdict.
