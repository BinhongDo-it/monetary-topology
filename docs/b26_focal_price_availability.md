# B26 availability: focal price points under a common cost shock

**Status: availability only. The carrier opens, with one half of the price side
recoverable and the other half still missing.** An earlier draft of this file
closed it. That verdict was wrong and the reason it was wrong is recorded in §3a
because the mistake is repeatable.

---

## 1. What it would have tested

One firm, one menu, one cost shock. The hypothesis in question predicts that a
**posted focal price point is rigid while the quantity behind it adjusts**, and
that non-focal prices on the same menu are not rigid.

That contrast needs no cross-country and no cross-firm variation. Cost, period,
firm and menu are all held identical, and the only thing that varies is whether a
given item's price is a focal point. **The design also carries its own positive
control**: if non-focal prices are equally rigid, the reading has no power and
says nothing, which is the outcome that must be reachable for the exercise to be
worth running at all.

---

## 2. Cost side: available, precise, and long

Arabica futures are exchange-traded, daily, and run for decades. The shock in
question is large and dated: roughly **70 per cent over 2024**, with the C-type
contract up close to **90 per cent over six months** and reaching **429.95 US
cents per pound**, its highest in decades.

A published unit cost decomposition for one operator in this market puts raw
material at **5.7** of **11.1** yuan per cup, with packaging `1.5`, labour `1.9`,
utilities `0.2` and rent `1.8`. **Raw material is therefore about half of unit
cost**, so a 70 to 90 per cent move in the bean price moves total unit cost by
roughly a third to two fifths. That is a large shock by any standard and its
timing is not in dispute.

---

## 3. Price side: closed

**The posted menu is an app, not a document, and it is not archived.** Point
observations exist in trade press: one focal point held at `9.9` while its
coverage was cut from daily to one coupon per week, its eligible products from
twenty to eight, and finally the named zone disappeared; two individual items
moved, `29` to `32` and `11` to `16`, over the same period. **Those are points,
not a series**, and a criterion that counts how many focal points moved against
how many non-focal points moved needs the whole menu at several dates.

**The realized average price is recoverable, but not from the press release.**
The quarterly release carries monthly average transacting customers, same-store
sales growth, self-operated store revenue and store-level profit, and it does not
carry cups. **Transacting customers is not transactions**, so revenue divided by
it is spend per customer per month, which is price times visit frequency times
basket size. **Basket size has little dispersion in this product**, one cup for
one person and two for two, **while frequency does, and frequency is precisely
what a promotional price point moves.** So the customer count is the wrong
denominator, and the reason is frequency rather than basket size.

**The right denominator is disclosed on the earnings call.** For 2023 management
stated "the number of total items sold in 2023 exceeding 2 billion, with the
number of freshly made beverages sold surpassed 1.9 billion cups". Against full
year net revenue of `24.9` billion yuan that is approximately **`13.1` yuan per
cup**. A trade publication independently reports the same quantity at other
dates: `11` yuan in 2020, `17` to `18` in 2021, and `13` in the fourth quarter of
2024. **The series exists and is partly already computed by others.**

### 3a. Why the first pass called this closed, since the mistake is repeatable

**The press release is not the disclosure.** Management routinely states on the
call figures the release omits, and a feasibility check that reads only the
release returns a false negative. The check must cover the release, the call
transcript, and the annual report, and the transcripts for this operator are
posted on its own investor site.

**What remains missing is the menu, not the average.** Cups give the realized
average price. They do not give which items were at the focal point on which
date, and that is still the classification problem of §3.

### 3b. The enumeration, run properly this time, and what it turned up

Six source classes, checked rather than assumed.

| class | result |
|---|---|
| press release | no cups, no menu |
| earnings call transcript | **cups, stated by management** |
| broker research | **an average selling price for the competitor, `11.58` yuan**, plus scattered posted and coupon prices. No systematic menu table |
| trade press | **points on the realized-average series**, and dated specifics of the coverage change |
| the seller's own social channel | current menu; whether historical promotional posts persist is unchecked |
| restaurant-data vendors | store counts confirmed; menu coverage unchecked |

**The coverage change has a dated before and after.** From June 2023 the focal
point applied to **all freshly made coffee drinks**; from February 2024 it applied
to **ten named drinks**, with the frequency moved from standing to periodic. The
chairman had stated in August 2023 that the focal point would be "normalised, for
at least two years".

**And the enumeration turned up something that matters more than the data.** A
store manager, quoted in trade press, describes the mechanism directly: the offer
is "sometimes ten yuan, sometimes fifteen, and there are pick-one-of-ten and
pick-one-of-eight variants, all set automatically by the system." Customer service
states that the scope, the participating stores and the eligible drinks may all be
adjusted.

**So there is no single national menu state to classify.** The terms vary by
store, by period and by customer, and they are set algorithmically. **This is a
threat to the criterion and it is recorded before any collection is paid for.**

**It also cuts the other way, and both readings should be kept.** The headline
number stays `9.9` while store, period, eligibility, frequency and even the
realized amount all move underneath it. That is the hypothesis's own sentence
restated as an operating description. **Restated is not measured**, and a design
resting on the store manager's quote would be resting on a description of the
mechanism rather than on a reading of it.

**And the classification is not archived either, which is the harder half.**
"Focal" cannot be defined as "the price that did not move" without the answer
being forced by the construction. The intended non-circular definition was the
firm's own named promotional zone, an external label the analyst does not choose.
**That membership list is exactly what changed over the window and exactly what
is not preserved.**

## 4. The pattern this is the second instance of

[`b25_price_band_availability.md`](b25_price_band_availability.md) reached the
same wall from the other side: the cost series it needed was either in the wrong
caliber when free, or behind a vendor paywall when in the right caliber.

**Two carriers, one shape.** In both, the cost side is a traded commodity with a
long public record, and the price side is either an app, a negotiated invoice, or
a vendor product. **The quantity that identifies the mechanism is a posted price
through time, and posted prices through time are the thing nobody keeps.**

**The realized average is the exception and it is worth stating separately.**
Where a seller is listed, revenue is reported and volume is stated somewhere in
the disclosure package, the realized average price is recoverable even when no
menu is. That does not replace the menu, because the whole contrast is between
one price that held and others that did not, and an average cannot separate them.
**It does supply the outer envelope**: if the realized average moved while a
focal point provably held, the movement happened somewhere else on the menu, and
that is a weaker version of the same reading obtainable without the menu.

That is a statement about what is recorded rather than about the mechanism, and
it should be read as narrowing the search rather than as evidence either way.

---

## 5. What a carrier has to have, stated so the next one is checked first

1. **Posted prices archived by a third party at several dates**, not
   reconstructed from press coverage.
2. **A classification of focal against non-focal that is external to the
   analyst** and archived alongside the price.
3. A cost shock that is dated, large relative to unit cost, and not chosen by the
   seller.

Physical goods sold on the large Chinese e-commerce platforms meet the first
condition: third-party price-history services archive listing prices over time
for those platforms. Whether they meet the second is unknown and is the first
thing to check on any successor carrier, **before any cost series is obtained**,
because the cost side has now twice been the easy half and twice been the wrong
half to secure first.

---

## 6. What this document does not claim

**Does not claim** that focal price rigidity was tested and not found. It was not
tested, and the earlier draft of this file wrongly said the carrier could not be
opened at all. Those are opposite signals to a later reader and the distinction is the
point of writing this down.

**Does not claim** that the cost shock had no effect. Two individual items are
recorded as having moved, and the coverage behind the focal point is recorded as
having been cut. Whether that pattern holds across a menu is the untested part.

**Does not claim** that the operator's disclosure is deficient. It discloses what
its reporting obligations require. The quantity this design needs is simply not
among them.
