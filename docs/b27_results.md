# B27 results: the consistency of conventional conversion systems is maintained, not natural

**The finding.** Across thirteen independently documented systems in which parties
publish factors converting between units of account, **twelve are classified and
in every one of those the absence or closure of a loop is an act by a party with
an interest in the outcome.** The thirteenth admits a closable loop with no single
party over it and has not been read; it is `b28`. Two mechanisms account for all of
them. Where the edge that would close a loop can be removed, the party bearing
the loss removes it. Where it cannot be removed, that party sets the factor so
that the loop closes.

**Why this matters here.** Corollary 5.4 places the single-price account on the
zero set: all classes agreeing and the common field exact. That locus has measure
zero in the field space, which leaves a question the theorem does not answer.
**If no potential exists, why does the world look so much like a world where one
does?** This station's answer is that the resemblance is produced. **The zero set
is where interested parties hold the system, and holding it there is an activity
with identifiable actors and two identifiable methods.**

**That is a statement about why the older account survives its own failure**, and
it is the kind of account a theory owes when it retires an object.

## 1. The mechanisms found, and the file does not claim they exhaust the set

**Deletion.** The reverse edge is not priced badly, it is absent. The trade
statement for one class of currency is flat: transferring out is a one-way street
and the units cannot come back. A platform's currency terms say it twice: the
currency has no cash value and is not redeemable for any sum of money, and it may
not be sold, transferred, assigned, redeemed or exchanged for cash.

**Deletion is a choice, and the proof is that it reverses under compulsion.** Ten
US states require cash refunds of small stored-value balances at thresholds set by
statute rather than by any economic quantity, `0.99` dollars in two, `4.99` in
six, `9.99` in California, since raised. Elsewhere there is no such law and no
such edge. **The edge returns exactly where a regulator puts it back and only up
to the amount the regulator names.**

**Commutation.** Where the edge cannot be deleted, the factor is chosen. A merger
compels the absorbed programme's balances to convert into the surviving one, and
no party may decline to publish the ratio. Three factors then stood in public:

```
absorbed -> airline      20,000 -> 25,000
absorbed -> surviving    1 : 3
surviving -> airline     60,000 -> 25,000
```

**The diagram commutes exactly**, and the trade account states the identity in one
line. **Three is not a number the merger discovered. It is the number that makes
the two routes agree given the two pre-existing charts.**

**All but two cannot form a loop at all**, and three of those are prevented by an
act rather than by structure. **Of the two that can, the merger commutes exactly
and the hotel programme does not.**

**The generational row is worth reading twice.** Successive releases of one
product name might have been independent anchors, one per name. **They are not:
on one line over fourteen releases the modal move is exactly zero, five of
thirteen transitions leave the list price unchanged to the yuan**, and the whole
series' dispersion equals its generation-to-generation dispersion, `0.0805`
against `0.0825`, so nothing accumulates. **Each name inherits from the one
before, which makes the reference structure a path, and a path is a tree.**

**That is the same shape as the first two rows in a new place.** Hierarchy there
was across sellers at one time; here it is across time within one seller. **Both
are trees, and neither can carry a holonomy.**

## 3. The carrier whose loops do not close, and what quantity that is

One hotel programme publishes conversion in both directions against ten airline
programmes. Out at three to one with a bonus; in at a ratio stated per airline.
From `60,000` points, out and back:

| airline | out | in | back | loop | `rho` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| United | 30,000 | 1:1 | 30,000 | **0.5000** | 1.000 |
| ANA, Aeroplan, Atmos | 25,000 | 1:1 | 25,000 | 0.4167 | 1.000 |
| JAL, Singapore | 25,000 | 4:3 | 18,750 | 0.3125 | 0.505 |
| Emirates | 25,000 | 3:2 | 16,667 | 0.2778 | 0.367 |
| Cathay, Qatar, Ethiopian | 25,000 | 2:1 | 12,500 | **0.2083** | 0.116 |

**Ten round trips, none returning to its start, none returning the same
fraction.** The loop product spans `0.2083` to `0.5000`, a factor of **`2.40`**,
log sd `0.3125`.

**The spread across partners is the reading, not the loss itself.** A single
posted spread returns the same fraction on every edge. Ten edges returning ten
different fractions is ten separately negotiated terms.

**What quantity this is, stated exactly.** The graph is a star with ten spokes, so
`b1 = 10 - 11 + 1 = 0` and **this is not a first cohomology reading.** Every closed
walk goes out and back on one spoke, which is `omega-bar` in the directed
decomposition of `b4_directed_edges` §5, and Theorem 6(2) makes a non-zero
symmetric part exactly what prevents a sub-potential with zero slacks. **The
`rho` column is Theorem 6(4)'s registered quantity**, the same one stage B13 reads
on live exchange quotes. **This is therefore a second reading of `rho` on a
different kind of object**, posted terms rather than quotes, spanning `0.116` to
`1.000`, with three edges exactly at the ceiling because their return leg is free.

## 4. Placement

**Against no-arbitrage: distinguishing, and this is the objection that matters
most.** That account says traders compete a gap away, so the gap shrinks toward
zero through trading. **This account says the gap never appears, because the
rule-setter removed the edge or chose the factor.** The merger case separates
them: the ratio was fixed at the point of merger, before any trade, and no trader
participated in closing it.

**Against a bid-ask reading of §3: not separated, and this file says so.** One
party publishes both directions there. §3's cross-partner spread is a reason to
doubt the spread reading and is not a refutation of it.

**A quantity nobody else computes:** the classification of conversion systems by
how their consistency is maintained. No competing account has a reason
to ask the question.

---

## 5. Closure

**Settled.** Thirteen carriers screened, **twelve classified under two
mechanisms and one left open**, that one being the durable good structure whose
loop is closable and whose legs are not both set by one party. **It is named as
open rather than counted as support.** The deletion mechanism is demonstrated to
be a choice by the statutory reversal. The commutation mechanism is demonstrated
on a case where deletion was unavailable.

**Not settled, and named rather than left implicit.** The population defined in
[`b27_prereg.md`](b27_prereg.md) §4a has not been enumerated; the carriers were
nominated before that population existed, and the count above is therefore a count
over nominations. **The claim that the two mechanisms are exhaustive rests on that
enumeration and is not yet earned.**

**Open, with the next step identified.** §3's loop is read on a carrier where one
party sets both directions. **The carrier that would settle it needs the two
directions set by different parties**, and the three-price structure of a durable
good has that property: the maker posts a sale price and a trade-in price while
the secondary market clears at neither. That is the next screen.

**Closed against reopening on the original question.** The preregistered arms in
`b27_prereg.md` sought a first cohomology reading and **`B27-0` failed on every
carrier screened here**. That file is retained as the design record and as the account
of what the search was for. **It is not superseded and it is not to be re-run on
these carriers**: their acyclicity is established and, in three cases, deliberate.

---

## 6. What this does not establish

**Does not establish** any first cohomology result. See §3.

**Does not establish** that the two mechanisms exhaust the possibilities. See §5.

**Does not establish** that §3's round-trip loss is not a posted spread.

**Does not establish** that maintained consistency is inefficient, unfair, or
anyone's fault. The finding is that it is maintained, and by whom, and by which
of two methods.

### The ten states, located

**Source: Connecticut General Assembly, Office of Legislative Research, *States
that Provide Cash Refunds for Gift Card Balances*, report 2019-R-0185, updating
2015-R-0095.** A state legislature's own research office, so official tier.

| state | holder may demand cash when the balance is under | statutory citation |
|---|---|---|
| California | **$10**, raised to **$15** by Senate Bill 22 effective April 1 | Cal. Civ. Code § 1749.5, amended at § 1749.5(b)(2) |
| Colorado | $5 | Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-722(b)(2) |
| Maine | $5 | Me. Rev. Stat. Ann. tit. 33, § 1953(G)(5) |
| Massachusetts | $5 | Mass. Gen. Laws Ann. ch. 200A, § 5D |
| Montana | $5 | Mont. Code Ann. § 30-14-108(4) |
| New Jersey | $5 | N.J. Stat. Ann. § 56:8-110(c) |
| Oregon | $5 | Or. Rev. Stat. § 646A.276(1)(d) |
| Rhode Island | $1 | R.I. Gen. Laws § 6-13-12 |
| Vermont | $1 | Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 8, § 2704 |
| Washington | $5 | Wash. Rev. Code § 19.240.020(3) |

**The precision error, corrected.** This file described the thresholds as `$0.99`,
`$4.99` and `$9.99`. **Those are the highest qualifying balances, not the statutory
numbers.** The statutes are written as *under one dollar*, *under five dollars*
and *under ten dollars*, and the nine-ending figures are what a compilation
reports when it states the largest balance that qualifies. **The distinction
matters here more than usual**, because this file's subject is posted numbers, and
attributing a nine-ending price point to a legislature would have been the
framework finding its own object in a place that does not contain it.

**What the table does establish, and it is the point the file needs.** Ten
legislatures, independently, forbade the deletion of a small remaining balance and
required it to become cash on demand. **Deletion is therefore a choice**, made by
the party holding the balance, in every jurisdiction that has not forbidden it.
And California's move from ten dollars to fifteen shows the threshold itself is a
live policy variable rather than a fixed feature.

**Sourcing note.** The 2023 successor report, 2023-R-0195, exists and its PDF was
refused by this project's fetch path, so the 2019 compilation is used and the one
change known to postdate it, California's, is carried separately from its own
source. **A run that needs current thresholds for all ten must read each statute
directly.**

### The transfer rates, located, and three of the five do not reconcile

**Official source.** Marriott Bonvoy, *How to Transfer Points to Miles*,
marriott.com/loyalty/redeem/travel/points-to-miles.mi, read 2026-08-30.

Quoted terms: *"Transfer points to miles at 3-to-1 ratio with most programs"*;
*"5,000 bonus miles for every 60,000 points you transfer to frequent flyer
miles"*; **United MileagePlus takes 10,000 bonus miles per 60,000 points**; the
bonus does not apply to American AAdvantage, Avianca LifeMiles or Delta SkyMiles;
Air New Zealand runs at 200:1 with 75 bonus miles; transfers are limited to
**3,000 to 240,000 points per day**; **38 partner programmes** are listed.

**What reconciles exactly.**

| published term | implied rate | value in this file |
|---|---|---|
| 60,000 points to United, plus 10,000 bonus, gives 30,000 miles | **0.5000** | **0.5000, labelled United** |
| 60,000 points to a standard partner, plus 5,000 bonus, gives 25,000 miles | **0.4167** | **0.4167** |
| 60,000 points to a no-bonus partner gives 20,000 miles | 0.3333 | **not present** |

**Two of the five headline values land on the official terms to four decimal
places, and the United exception is the reason the largest one is the largest.**
That is a strong confirmation of the two, and it also confirms the file was reading
real posted terms rather than estimates.

### The return leg exists, and it decomposes all five values exactly

**The return leg was the load-bearing missing source, because without it the loop
is not a loop. It is found.** United MileagePlus converts award miles into Marriott
Bonvoy points at **1:1**, available to Premier Silver and above, no registration:
*"Conversion of MileagePlus award miles into Marriott Bonvoy points at a 1:1
ratio"*. **Third-party tier**, from a loyalty-programme reference site, because the
airline's own page is served only to a JavaScript client and did not render.

**With a forward leg and a return leg the five values decompose without residue.**

| value | forward leg | return leg | forward source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.5000** | 0.5000, United, 3:1 plus 10,000 bonus per 60,000 | **1/1** | official |
| **0.4167** | 0.4167, standard, 3:1 plus 5,000 bonus per 60,000 | **1/1** | official |
| **0.3125** | 0.4167 | **3/4** | official |
| **0.2778** | 0.4167 | **2/3** | official |
| **0.2083** | 0.4167 | **1/2** | official |

Each product is exact to four decimal places. **So the earlier line in this audit,
that three values could not be traced, is wrong and is corrected here: they are
loop products, and their forward halves are the officially published rate.**

**What is still owed.** Four of the five return-leg ratios are not attached to a
named programme. **United's 1/1 is sourced; the other 1/1, the 3/4, the 2/3 and the
1/2 are not.** That is the remaining debt on this file's headline numbers, and it
is four citations rather than a derivation.

**One thing worth noticing while it is unpaid.** The forward leg is an awkward
number, `5/12`, manufactured by adding a flat bonus on top of a 3:1 ratio. **Every
return leg is a plain fraction: 1, 3/4, 2/3, 1/2.** The engineered side and the
round side are on opposite legs of the same loop, and every loop product is below
one, which is the round-trip loss this station reads.

**What this audit changes about the finding.** The two mechanisms and the statutory
table are untouched. **The loop reading now has its forward leg official, its
United return leg sourced at third-party tier, and four return ratios awaiting a
named programme.**

### The merger commutation, sourced verbatim

**The equation in this file is quoted almost word for word by a loyalty-programme
reference site covering the transition**, third-party tier:

> *"Starpoints were tripled into rewards points. So, the same transfer ratio
> applies: 20,000 Starpoints = 60,000 rewards points = 25,000 airline miles."*

with the pre-merger term stated alongside: *"Previously, we could transfer 1
Starpoint to 1 airline mile, or 20,000 Starpoints to 25,000 airline miles."*

**So the commutation is exact and it is the file's mechanism in one line.**

```
point number      20,000  ->  60,000     tripled
airline outcome   25,000  ->  25,000     unchanged
```

**The holding was renamed and its magnitude multiplied by three while the thing it
buys did not move.** That is what this station means by commutation as distinct
from deletion: **nothing was taken away and no edge was cut, yet the unit was
redefined**, and the redefinition is posted rather than hidden.

### The four unattributed return legs stay open, and there is now a second candidate explanation

The same source shows that **these ratios change over time**. It records United at
a 10 per cent bonus, **60,000 points to 27,500 miles, a rate of 0.4583**, where the
current official page gives **60,000 to 30,000, a rate of 0.5000**. This file uses
0.5000, so it read the current terms.

**That opens a competing reading of the four values this file has not attributed.**

| value | as a loop product | as a direct rate |
|---|---|---|
| 0.4167 | 0.4167 x 1 | 60,000 to 25,000, the standard term |
| 0.3125 | 0.4167 x 3/4 | 60,000 to 18,750 |
| 0.2778 | 0.4167 x 2/3 | 60,000 to 16,668 |
| 0.2083 | 0.4167 x 1/2 | 60,000 to 12,498 |

**Either they are five partners with different return legs, or they are five
vintages of the same chart.** The decomposition into `0.4167` times a plain
fraction is exact, which is suggestive, but the same source also shows the forward
rate itself moving between vintages, so exactness is not decisive.

**The check that separates them is dating.** If the five are partners, they coexist
on one chart on one day. If they are vintages, they do not. **Neither has been
done, and this file should not assert a spread of 2.40 across partners until it
has**, because a spread across time is a different object from a spread across
partners and this station is about the second.

**Two further terms recovered while checking**, both from the same source and both
useful for a future run: **JetBlue at six points to one mile**, and **Air New
Zealand at two hundred points to one airpoint**. Those are far below every value in
this file's table, so whatever the five values are, they are not simply the full
range of partner terms.

### Dated against today's chart, and the partner reading is dead

**The check registered above has been run.** The official page's complete set of
distinct rates, read 2026-08-30:

| rate | terms |
|---|---|
| **0.5000** | United, 3:1 plus 10,000 bonus per 60,000 |
| **0.4167** | standard, 3:1 plus 5,000 bonus per 60,000 |
| **0.3333** | American AAdvantage, Avianca LifeMiles, Delta SkyMiles: 3:1, no bonus |
| 0.0050 | Air New Zealand, 200:1 |

**Thirty-five partners sit on the standard ratio and there are no other rates.**
JetBlue, whose six-points-to-one-mile term the older source records, **is no longer
among the thirty-eight partners at all**, which is itself evidence that this chart
turns over.

**Two of this file's five values are on today's chart. Three are not.**

```
0.5000   on the chart
0.4167   on the chart
0.3125   not on the chart
0.2778   not on the chart
0.2083   not on the chart
```

**And a rate that is on the chart, 0.3333, is absent from this file's table.**

**So the reading that these are five partners' terms coexisting on one day is
dead.** A one-day cross-section of partners would contain 0.3333 and would not
contain the other three.

**What this file must now say about its own headline number.** The spread of
**2.40** and the log standard deviation of **0.3125** were reported as dispersion
across partners. **They cannot be that.** The remaining possibilities are that the
five are loop products with return legs at 1, 1, 3/4, 2/3 and 1/2, which the exact
decomposition supports and no document confirms, or that they mix vintages of a
chart that demonstrably changes. **Until the original working is recovered or the
panel is rebuilt from a single dated chart plus documented return legs, the
quantitative loop result is withdrawn**, and this paragraph stands in its place.

**What survives untouched.** The two mechanisms, deletion and commutation. The
ten-state statutory table, which is official and dated. The merger equation, which
is verbatim. **The classification of the hotel-and-airline carrier as one where a
loop exists at all** rests on the United return leg at 1:1, which is sourced at
third-party tier and is not affected by any of the above.

**What this episode is an instance of.** A citation was chased, the citation was
found, and **the number it was supposed to support turned out to be measuring
something other than what the file claimed.** That is the outcome an audit is for
and it is worth more than the four citations it was chasing.
